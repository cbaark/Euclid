# Euclid User Manual

Euclid is a project management tool built for one person: a solo or student developer who's tired of juggling a notes app, a separate bug tracker, a Word doc for requirements, and a pile of browser bookmarks. Everything lives in one place, and it works even when you don't have a connection.

## Getting started

**Register:** go to `/register`, choose a username and password (8+ characters, at least one uppercase letter and one digit), and confirm your password. You're taken straight to your dashboard.

**Log in:** go to `/login` with your username and password.

The sidebar on the left is always there once you're logged in, every module is one click away from any page.

## Dashboard

Your home screen. Shows quick stats across all four modules (journal entries, urgent bugs, requirements completed, references saved) and a recent activity feed of your last 5 actions system-wide, so you can see where you left off without opening anything else.

## Developer Journal

Your daily log. Each entry records what you worked on, the date, an entry type (e.g. Daily Log), and any notes. This is the place to write down decisions as you make them, the whole point is that you don't lose track of *why* you did something three weeks later.

## Bug Tracker (Kanban)

A three-column board, Urgent, To-Do, Completed, for tracking issues and tasks. Drag a card between columns to change its status, or open it to edit the title and priority directly.

## Requirements Tracker

Define and track your project's scope. Each requirement is auto-tagged as Functional (FR-001, FR-002…) or Non-Functional (NFR-001…) as you add them, so you always have a clear, numbered list of what the system is supposed to do.

## Reference List

Save links to documentation, tutorials, or anything else you looked up during development, so you're not digging through browser history to find that one Stack Overflow answer again.

## Working offline

If you lose your connection, Euclid keeps working. Anything you create or edit while offline is queued locally and automatically synced to the server the next time you're back online, nothing is lost, and you don't need to do anything manually to trigger the sync.
